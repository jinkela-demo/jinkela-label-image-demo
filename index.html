Principles

A company of one runs on leverage, not effort.

One person can't do everything, so picking the right leverage comes first. These six rules are how I cut scope and make decisions every day.
Leverage first
Capital, code, content and AI — four forms of leverage nobody needs permission to use. For every task, ask whether a system could run it again.
Minimum viable company
Find one paying customer before you find a name. The company is an outcome, not a starting point.
Profitable by default
Cash-flow positive from the first invoice. Without funding, profit is the only safety net a solo company has.
Systems over effort
Anything done three times becomes a checklist, a script or a template. Spend your hours only on judgement that can't be automated.
Build in public
Turn the process into content. Readers are the best customer research, hiring channel and co-founder filter you'll ever have.
A sustainable pace
Capacity comes from consistency, not heroic sprints. Four focused hours a day beats three sleepless weeks.
The path

From one person to one company.

Six steps, from my first client project to automated delivery. Every one of them is doable alone.
01

Find deliverable value

Spend a week writing down who you help, what you solve, and what you get in return. If you can't write it, don't register the company yet.

02

Sell the first one

Delivering by hand is fine. Get paid once — what you're validating isn't the product, it's that the need is real.

03

Package it

Turn the repeatable parts of the service into templates, packages or a subscription. Price once, deliver forever.

04

Build the pipeline

Forms, payments, delivery, invoices and support replies all go to the toolchain. The goal is zero manual handoffs.

05

Grow with content

Publish every mistake as a note. Distribution needs no budget, only consistency and honesty.

06

Let it run

Watch three numbers a month — revenue, gross margin, and the share of time the system absorbs. Everything else can be deleted.
